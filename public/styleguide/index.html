Winslow Pantry brand style guide

Internal use only. This page is password protected, hidden from search engines and not linked from the public site. Please do not share the link outside the team.

Winslow Pantry is inspired by the charm of the local farmer's market and a simpler life, bringing a fresh, inviting spirit to the heart of the home. Rooted in the bounty of color and new sensations that come with the seasons, our kitchen collection blends timeless charm with everyday function.

This guide translates the printed Brand Guidelines into rules for the website: the logo, colors, type, patterns and packaging from the guide, plus the accessibility, copy and translation standards the site is built on.

Logo

Color variations

Clear space

Keep a clear space of half the logo height (½ H) on every side. Nothing else (text, edges, photos or patterns) should enter that area.

On the website the header logo is never smaller than 108px wide, and the badge versions are never set below 72px wide, so the script "Pantry" stays readable.

Please don't

  • Stretch, skew or rotate the logo.
  • Recolor it outside the ink, white and badge versions shown here.
  • Place the outline logo directly on a busy fruit print. Use a badge version instead.
  • Rebuild the word mark in a font. Always use the artwork files.

Accessibility and color vision

The brand palette is soft and pastel. These rules keep it exactly as designed while making sure every visitor, including people with color blindness or low vision, can read and use the site.

Contrast pairings

Ratings follow WCAG 2.2. AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text (24px and up, or 19px bold) and for icons and outlines. Each result is spelled out in words and marked with a symbol, so it never depends on color.

Rules the site follows

  • Never use color alone to carry meaning. Finishes and product colors are always named in text ("oat, wheat, lake blue, navy and stone").
  • Links are underlined in running text, and the current page in the menu is marked with a heavy underline and a light pill, not only a color change.
  • Body text is always Ink (or white on Harbor and Ink). Pastel brand colors are backgrounds, never text.
  • Harbor carries buttons with white text (5.2:1). For small link text on cream we use Harbor deep, a darker step of the same hue.
  • Avoid red and green side by side to tell things apart. In internal materials the cherry and lime prints are always labeled.
  • Focus is always visible: a 3px Ink outline with a white halo (reversed on dark sections).
  • Respect user settings: reduced motion turns off transitions, higher contrast removes soft tints, and forced colors keep button outlines.

Motion

Motion should feel like a slow Saturday morning at the market. It adds warmth, never urgency.

Principles

  • Set down, then settle. Things arrive like goods placed on a market table: a soft drop with a small overshoot, never a snap.
  • Unhurried. Most moves take 0.6 to 1.3 seconds and are staggered so the eye can follow one thing at a time.
  • Borrowed from the market. Tags sway on their pegs, fruit rolls into place, wood slices turn, the plaid drifts as you scroll.
  • Quiet when idle. Ambient sways play once and come to rest within five seconds (WCAG 2.2.2).
  • Optional. Visitors who ask for reduced motion get the same page with no animation, and every element is visible without JavaScript.

Try it

Where it is used

  • Page curtain: the cornflower cover with the plaid edge and badge rises when you follow a link and lifts away on the next page.
  • Hero: the script kicker writes itself in, the headline rises word by word, the packages are set down and sway.
  • Scroll: section headings and copy rise in, product cards are laid out one by one, material swatches turn, the plaid drifts and fruit spots float with gentle parallax.
  • Collection: each product panel opens like an arch window, and the jump menu marks the product in view.
  • Interface: FAQ answers open smoothly, and the mobile menu and language menu ease in.

Built with GSAP 3.13 and ScrollTrigger, self hosted in public/assets/js/vendor/. All motion lives in public/assets/js/motion.js.

Voice and copy

How we sound

  • Warm and neighborly, like the person behind the farm stand.
  • Concrete. Name real foods, real moments and real materials.
  • Short sentences. Plain words. One idea at a time.
  • Honest. Only claim what the product and packaging support.

House rules

  • No em dashes or en dashes. Use a period, a comma or a colon instead. The build fails if one appears in the site copy.
  • No filler words like "elevate", "curated", "seamless" or "game changer".
  • No "Whether you're..." openers or stacked lists of three adjectives.
  • No exclamation marks in headlines.

Avoid Elevate your kitchen with curated tools that seamlessly blend form and function.

Write Honest materials, cheerful color and tools that earn their spot by the stove.

Translation

The site is published in English, Español, Français. Every word of public copy lives in src/i18n/<language>.json; the templates contain no text of their own.

  1. Copy src/i18n/en.json to a new file named with the language code, for example src/i18n/de.json.
  2. Translate the values (never the keys). Anything left out falls back to English, and the build lists what is missing.
  3. Add the language to "locales" in src/site.json with its name, hreflang and Open Graph locale.
  4. Run npm run build and npm run og. Pages, hreflang tags, the sitemap, the language menu and the share images are all generated.

Visitors are never redirected. If their browser prefers a language we publish, a small banner offers it once and remembers the choice.

SEO and sharing

Every page has a unique title (60 characters or fewer), a meta description (160 or fewer), a canonical URL, hreflang alternates, Open Graph and Twitter tags, structured data and a 1200 × 630 share image in its own language. The build checks the lengths below.
